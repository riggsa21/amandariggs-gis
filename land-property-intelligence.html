Stormwater retention loss · soils · development scenarios

Understand the retention capacity a development may remove.

GSI is developing a geospatial decision-support workflow to compare existing landscape retention with proposed development, identify where loss is concentrated, estimate added event runoff, and follow likely surface pathways beyond the site boundary.

Core question

If this land is developed, what changes?

The planned system is built around a simple planning question: how much stormwater-retention capacity is lost, where does that loss occur, and where might the additional runoff move?

Baseline

Existing retention

Combine soils, hydrologic soil group, land cover, imperviousness, topography, and selected rainfall conditions to establish a reproducible baseline.

Scenario

Proposed development

Apply proposed buildings, roads, parking, and other impervious changes to the same landscape and recalculate event response.

Difference

Retention deficit

Map and summarize the modeled difference between existing and proposed conditions rather than reporting impervious change alone.

Soil-forward analysis

Do not hide the soil underneath the map.

Soil behavior is central to the retention question. GSI's development roadmap emphasizes hydrologic soil groups and mapped soil properties while also pursuing opportunities to validate model assumptions with depth-specific hydraulic measurements.

Mapped soil inputs

  • Hydrologic soil group
  • Drainage class
  • Saturated hydraulic conductivity where suitable
  • Texture and restrictive layers
  • Available soil-depth information
  • Land cover and impervious condition

Validation direction

  • Depth- or horizon-specific sampling
  • Hydraulic conductivity measurements
  • Soil-water retention characteristics
  • Comparison of mapped and measured conditions
  • Documented uncertainty
  • Model refinement through observed cases
Beyond the parcel

Retention loss matters because water keeps moving.

A central GSI goal is to connect modeled retention loss to likely downstream surface-flow pathways and receptors, while clearly separating terrain-based screening from engineering-grade hydraulic analysis.

01

Loss hotspots

Locate areas where proposed change creates the largest modeled loss.

02

Outlets

Identify where additional surface runoff is likely to leave the development area.

03

Pathways

Follow terrain-derived routes through the surrounding landscape.

04

Receptors

Screen roads, parcels, buildings, streams, wetlands, and other mapped features downstream.

How the existing tools fit

The retention platform grows out of GSI's site-screening workflow.

Initial screening, terrain/soils analysis, usable-area analysis, site-fit scenarios, planning context, and post-build drainage work remain active capabilities. They provide the real-world inputs and scenario structure the retention-loss system needs.

Alternative layouts or conceptual measures compared against the same baseline.

Scope and limitations: GSI retention-loss work is being developed as planning-level geospatial decision support. It is not a substitute for a boundary survey, geotechnical investigation, jurisdictional wetland determination, stamped engineering, drainage design, permit-specific hydrologic/hydraulic modeling, or regulatory approval. Confidence should be reported separately for soils, terrain, runoff estimation, routing, and receptor data.
