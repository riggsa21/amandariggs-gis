Environment

Review available wetlands, soils, vegetation and development, protected or sensitive environmental features, and other mapped conditions that may warrant closer professional or agency review.

Terrain

Access

Investigate visible access patterns, road context, nearby development, neighboring land uses, infrastructure, and how the property relates to nearby features that may not be obvious from listing photos alone.

History

How Property Research Works

A focused process built around your intended use and the decision in front of you.

Send the Property

Share an address, parcel number, listing link, coordinates, or a map. Include the intended use, the decision being considered, and any questions or concerns that should be investigated before moving forward.

Investigate the Evidence

GeoSite identifies useful information, researches the property with mapping tools, compares relevant physical and environmental conditions, and flags areas where the evidence suggests a closer look or additional verification.

Use the Findings

Receive the agreed-upon maps and research summary, including key observations, data limitations, and practical next questions. If field verification would add value, it can be planned from there.

What you can receive

Deliverables are scaled to the property and question rather than forced into a one-size-fits-all package.

  • Property research summary with focused findings in plain language.
  • Professional maps showing the conditions most relevant to the decision.
  • Early environmental and land review using appropriate available information.
  • Past-image observations when earlier landscape change is relevant.
  • Parcel comparison for buyers or investors choosing among multiple properties.
  • Data sources and limitations so you know what the evidence can and cannot establish.
  • Recommended next questions for agencies, surveyors, engineers, environmental professionals, or other specialists when needed.
  • Optional field documentation when on-site observations would strengthen the project.

Useful early research without pretending the map is the final authority.

Good pre-purchase research should help you identify what deserves deeper verification - not overstate what public data can prove.

GeoSite Intel provides map-based research, early review, field observations when included in the project, and professional mapping. GeoSite does not provide a boundary survey, title opinion, appraisal, engineering design or opinion, legal advice, permit approval, official flood determination, jurisdictional wetland delineation, Phase I Environmental Site Assessment, geotechnical evaluation, or guarantee that a property is buildable or suitable for a particular use. When research identifies a question outside GeoSite’s services, the findings can help you determine what type of official verification or licensed professional to contact next.

Property & Land Research FAQ

Can GeoSite research property outside the local area?

Yes. Most GeoSite property research can be completed remotely using available public records, maps, environmental data, imagery, terrain, and client-provided information. Projects can be considered across the United States and internationally where suitable data are available. The exact datasets and level of detail vary by location.

Can GeoSite determine whether land is buildable?

GeoSite can identify mapped conditions, possible concerns, and land-use questions that deserve verification. A legal decision about whether land can be built on can depend on zoning rules, permits, utilities, access rights, surveys, engineering, soils, septic requirements, wetlands determinations, and other local or site-specific factors that must be confirmed by the appropriate authority or professional.

What information should be sent?

An address, parcel number, listing link, coordinates, or a map is enough to start. The most useful additional information is the intended use of the property and any concerns already identified.

Is this only for vacant land?

No. Property research can also support questions involving homes, commercial parcels, agricultural land, investment property, access, drainage, surrounding development, historical change, and other location-based concerns.

Are site visits available?

Yes. When a project would benefit from on-site observations, photography, GPS data collection, or on-site checks, a field visit can be planned separately based on location, access, travel, and the specific information needed.

Found a property? Start with the location.

Send the parcel or listing along with the intended use or property question. GeoSite will review the request, determine what useful information is available, and recommend a focused research plan.

You can send: a Zillow, Realtor, LandWatch, Land.com, or other listing link; a street address; property ID; a map location; county property-map link; or even a screenshot or map showing the property.
