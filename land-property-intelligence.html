How Property Intelligence Works

A focused process built around your intended use and the decision in front of you.

Send the Property

Share an address, parcel number, listing link, coordinates, or a map. Include the intended use, the decision being considered, and any questions or concerns that should be investigated before moving forward.

Investigate the Evidence

GeoSite identifies useful data, researches the property in GIS, compares relevant physical and environmental conditions, and flags areas where the evidence suggests a closer look or additional verification.

Use the Findings

Receive the agreed-upon maps and research summary, including key observations, data limitations, and practical next questions. If field verification would add value, it can be scoped from there.

What you can receive

Deliverables are scaled to the property and question rather than forced into a one-size-fits-all package.

  • Property research summary with focused findings in plain language.
  • Professional GIS maps showing the conditions most relevant to the decision.
  • Environmental and terrain screening using appropriate available datasets.
  • Historical imagery observations when past landscape change is relevant.
  • Parcel comparison for buyers or investors choosing among multiple properties.
  • Data sources and limitations so you know what the evidence can and cannot establish.
  • Recommended next questions for agencies, surveyors, engineers, environmental professionals, or other specialists when needed.
  • Optional field documentation when on-site observations would strengthen the project.

Useful screening without pretending the map is the final authority.

Good land due diligence should help you identify what deserves deeper verification—not overstate what public data can prove.

GeoSite Intel provides GIS-based research, preliminary screening, field observations when specifically scoped, and professional mapping. GeoSite does not provide a boundary survey, title opinion, appraisal, engineering design or opinion, legal advice, permit approval, official flood determination, jurisdictional wetland delineation, Phase I Environmental Site Assessment, geotechnical evaluation, or guarantee that a property is buildable or suitable for a particular use. When research identifies a question outside GeoSite’s scope, the findings can help you determine what type of official verification or licensed professional to contact next.

Land & Property Intelligence FAQ

Can GeoSite research property outside the local area?

Yes. Most GeoSite property research can be completed remotely using available public, geospatial, environmental, imagery, terrain, and client-provided information. Projects can be considered across the United States and internationally where suitable data are available. The exact datasets and level of detail vary by location.

Can GeoSite determine whether land is buildable?

GeoSite can identify mapped conditions, potential constraints, and land-use questions that deserve verification. A legal buildability determination can depend on zoning interpretation, permits, utilities, access rights, surveys, engineering, soils, septic requirements, wetlands determinations, and other local or site-specific factors that must be confirmed by the appropriate authority or professional.

What information should be sent?

An address, parcel number, listing link, coordinates, or a map is enough to start. The most useful additional information is the intended use of the property and any concerns already identified.

Is this only for vacant land?

No. Property intelligence can also support questions involving homes, commercial parcels, agricultural land, investment property, access, drainage, surrounding development, historical change, and other location-based concerns.

Are site visits available?

Yes. When a project would benefit from on-site observations, photography, GPS data collection, or ground-truthing, a field visit can be scoped separately based on location, access, travel, and the specific information needed.
