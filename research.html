Published research → applied planning tools

The research foundation behind GeoSite's next direction.

GeoSite Intel's current retention-loss work grows directly from published research on urban development, stormwater retention, nutrient export, and landscape change.

Peer-reviewed publication

Assessing Nutrient Export and Stormwater Retention Amid Urban Growth in Greenville, North Carolina

Amanda Riggs, Steven M. Richter, and Beth Bee. Southeastern Geographer, 66(2), 122–142, 2026.

The study examined ecosystem-service change across five subwatersheds in Greenville, North Carolina over a 2001–2021 period, using land-use/land-cover information and InVEST modeling to evaluate stormwater retention and nutrient delivery as urban growth occurred.

Open published articleECU thesis record
Why continue the question?

The next problem is not whether urban growth changes runoff.

The applied research question is how much natural stormwater-retention capacity is lost at specific development locations, which soil and landscape conditions make that loss most consequential, where the added runoff is likely to move, and how communities can compare alternatives before construction.

Research question 1

Measure the loss

Quantify event-scale retention change at development and watershed scales.

Research question 2

Explain the soil

Test how mapped soil properties and hydrologic classifications correspond with measured hydraulic behavior.

Research question 3

Follow the consequence

Connect retention deficits to likely downstream pathways, receptors, and cumulative watershed change.

Current development work

From ecosystem-service finding to decision-support system.

Build a transparent baseline

Use authoritative soils, land cover, imperviousness, terrain, and rainfall inputs with documented assumptions.

Model proposed change

Apply development footprints and compare the same event under existing and proposed conditions.

Validate the soil assumptions

Pursue field/lab collaboration for hydraulic conductivity and soil-water retention measurements at selected study sites.

Validate against real outcomes

Compare predictions with observed post-development conditions where suitable records and field evidence are available.

Research collaboration: GSI is interested in partnerships that can improve soil characterization, hydrologic validation, development-scenario testing, and community-scale application. Current work is developmental; methods, uncertainty, and appropriate professional boundaries are intended to remain explicit throughout the process.
