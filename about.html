Research · geospatial systems · useful tools

GeoSite Intel exists to turn spatial research into better decisions.

GSI is a North Carolina-based geospatial research and tool-development company focused primarily on understanding how land development changes stormwater retention and downstream conditions—and on turning that science into useful decision support for communities.

Mission

Help communities see consequences earlier.

Development decisions are often made one project at a time, while soils, drainage, watersheds, and downstream impacts operate across property boundaries. GSI is working toward tools that make those relationships easier to evaluate before change is built into the landscape.

Founder

Amanda L. Riggs, M.S.

Amanda is a published geospatial and environmental researcher with an M.S. in Geography from East Carolina University. Her research has examined urban growth, ecosystem services, nutrient export, stormwater retention, land-cover change, and spatial modeling.

Primary direction

Stormwater retention loss & development impact.

The flagship GSI effort is a planning-level geospatial system that compares existing and proposed development conditions, emphasizes soil and retention behavior, estimates event-scale retention deficits and added runoff, and connects those changes to likely downstream pathways and receptors.

Additional capability

GSI also builds tools for businesses.

Custom automation, data-processing workflows, document utilities, QA systems, and lightweight internal tools remain part of GSI. The common thread is practical: build a system around a real problem, keep assumptions and quality controls visible, and make the output useful.
