About GeoSite Intel LLC

Built on geography, research, and practical problem solving.

GeoSite Intel combines method-driven spatial research with custom technical tools. Land intelligence and workflow automation are separate services under one recognizable brand.

Our foundation

Geography and analysis are not an afterthought.

GeoSite Intel was founded by Amanda L. Riggs, M.S. in Geography, with a background in GIS, cartography, spatial data analysis, Python scripting, environmental research, and technical reporting.

That research foundation supports the land and property analysis side of the business as well as the careful design and validation of GSI Workflow Studio tools.

GeoSite Intelligence focuses on land questions, environmental context, GIS research, and maps. GSI Workflow Studio focuses on repeatable business and geospatial workflows.

The GeoSite standard

  • Clear questions and defined scope
  • Appropriate sources and transparent assumptions
  • Reviewable results, not black-box claims
  • Explicit limitations and needed verification
  • Direct, email-first project communication
Research and publications

Published research that informs the work.

Academic work belongs with the founder's background, not as an unrelated commercial service. The publication below is cited as a scholarly credential, not presented as an endorsement by the coauthors of GeoSite Intel.

Peer-reviewed research / 2026

Assessing Nutrient Export and Stormwater Retention Amid Urban Growth in Greenville, North Carolina

Amanda Riggs, Steven M. Richter, and Beth Bee
Southeastern Geographer, 66(2), 122-142 (2026).

The study assessed how landscape change relates to stormwater retention and nutrient export across Greenville-area subwatersheds. The methods inform GeoSite's interest in geospatial decision support, reproducibility, and stated uncertainty.

Research direction

Development change and stormwater-retention questions

Current research interests include land-use change, retention-loss scenarios, possible downstream exposure, and uncertainty in planning-level GIS models. Experimental concepts are not represented as validated engineering products.

Another exploratory idea is a documented review of GIS dataset access, permission terms, and use restrictions before a project begins.
