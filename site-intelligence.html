Current GSI workflow

From initial screen to development scenario.

The site-intelligence tools remain active because they answer immediate development questions and supply the site-level structure needed for the larger retention-loss platform.

Phase 1

Initial screening

Parcel and surrounding context, flood and wetland indicators, environmental constraints, roads, nearby uses, and data gaps.

Phase 2

Terrain & soils

Elevation, slope, hydrologic soils, drainage characteristics, low areas, modeled flow paths, and terrain limitations.

Phase 3

Usable area

Combine mapped constraints, terrain, soils, planning information, and project needs to identify areas suitable for closer design review.

Phase 4

Site-fit scenarios

Place conceptual buildings, roads, parking, storage, subdivisions, or other footprints and compare alternative arrangements.

Drainage intelligence

Do not stop at the property line.

GSI's drainage workflow can derive terrain-based flow direction and accumulation, identify likely outlets, trace downstream pathways, and screen mapped receptors such as roads, wetlands, streams, buildings, and neighboring parcels.

Pre-development flow

Map existing terrain-derived drainage patterns and outlet structure.

Post-build scenario

Evaluate how proposed footprints may redirect or concentrate surface flow at a planning level.

Downstream context

Extend analysis through a larger buffer so potential consequences are not hidden by the parcel boundary.

Planning context

Physical feasibility is only part of the question.

Where reliable public information is available, site reviews can incorporate zoning, land-use context, overlays, access considerations, nearby development patterns, and potential regulatory or professional follow-up needs.

Connection to the flagship system

Every site study can help improve retention-loss modeling.

Site intelligence is where GSI tests data preparation, soil interpretation, scenario placement, drainage routing, receptor screening, QA, confidence reporting, and real-world development questions. The retention-loss platform adds a more explicit measure of what natural stormwater-retention capacity is lost and what that change may mean downstream.
