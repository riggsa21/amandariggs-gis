No GIS knowledge required

What does GIS actually mean here?

GIS stands for Geographic Information Systems. In practical terms, it is a way to combine maps with layers of location-based information—such as parcel boundaries, flood areas, wetlands, elevation, soils, roads, land cover, and historical imagery—so patterns and relationships become easier to see. GeoSite uses GIS as an investigative tool for land, then translates the findings into clear maps and plain-language context.

Plain-English guide

A few useful land-research terms

No technical vocabulary is required to request help. These short definitions explain the terms that may appear in a GeoSite review.

□

Parcel

A legally recorded piece of land. A parcel number identifies it in local property and tax records.

⊞

GIS

A digital mapping system used to compare information connected to the same location.

◎

Early Review

A first review of available maps and records to flag questions that may need closer professional follow-up.

≋

Terrain & Drainage

The shape and slope of the land and the likely direction water may move across it.

∴

LiDAR

Detailed elevation data created with laser measurements, often collected from an aircraft.

⌖

Field Check

An on-site visit used to compare mapped information with visible conditions on the ground.

Useful early research without overstating what a map can prove

Property research is designed to reveal what deserves attention and what should be verified next—not to replace licensed or official determinations.

GeoSite Intel provides map-based research, early review, professional mapping, and field observations when included in the project. Services do not replace a boundary survey, title opinion, appraisal, engineering evaluation, legal advice, permit approval, official flood determination, jurisdictional wetland delineation, Phase I Environmental Site Assessment, geotechnical evaluation, or other licensed or regulatory determination. GeoSite does not guarantee that a property is buildable or suitable for a particular use.
