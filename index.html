Current development focus: GSI is building a planning-level retention-loss and development-impact system that combines soils, land cover, terrain, proposed impervious change, event runoff, downstream routing, and explicit confidence reporting. The system is in development and is intended to complement—not replace—licensed engineering and permit-specific hydrologic/hydraulic analysis.
Flagship focus

Stormwater retention is a land-development decision.

Two development footprints of the same size can interact very differently with soils, topography, natural retention areas, and downstream systems. GSI is focused on making those differences visible early enough to inform planning.

Active service & model test bed

Site Intelligence & Feasibility stays.

GSI still evaluates real development sites. These projects are not separate from the retention mission—they provide the practical site conditions, scenarios, and QA needed to build a stronger planning system.

Screen

Initial site review

Parcel context, flood/wetland indicators, soils, environmental constraints, surrounding land use, and planning context.

Terrain

Terrain & drainage

DEM-based slope, low areas, modeled flow direction, accumulation, outlets, drainage buffers, and downstream context.

Feasibility

Usable area & constraints

Combine terrain, soils, setbacks, mapped constraints, and planning information to identify areas that deserve closer design review.

Scenario

Site-fit scenarios

Test conceptual buildings, parking, roads, storage, subdivisions, or other development footprints before moving into detailed design.

Research behind the work

Built from published research, not a marketing concept.

GeoSite Intel founder Amanda L. Riggs co-authored the 2026 peer-reviewed article Assessing Nutrient Export and Stormwater Retention Amid Urban Growth in Greenville, North Carolina in Southeastern Geographer. The study examined land-use change, nutrient export, and stormwater retention across five Greenville-area subwatersheds.
