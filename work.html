Independent demonstration 01

601 Fairview Avenue development scenario.

A phased QGIS workflow moved from initial parcel screening into terrain and soil analysis, usable-area review, a conceptual garage footprint, impervious-area comparison, and terrain-based drainage context.

The project also tested how a proposed footprint could be carried through repeatable analysis steps while keeping source layers, QA checks, and editable outputs organized for review.

Independent demonstration 02

0 Mills Road property intelligence report.

This completed 13-page screening brought parcel records, aerial imagery, wetlands, flood information, soils, terrain, drainage, access, surrounding activity, measured relationships, sources, and limitations into one decision-support report.

A nearby imagery-observed agricultural feature was documented and measured without overstating what the available evidence proved. The report separated observation, mapped evidence, interpretation, and recommended verification.

Independent demonstration 03

Outlet Chicod Creek watershed context.

Two related workflows examined how a parcel sits within the larger drainage landscape and how land cover changed across a consistent watershed area between 2001 and 2024.

The drainage analysis connected parcel-scale terrain signals to the surrounding network. The land-cover analysis used consistent 30-meter classification data to quantify major transitions while clearly noting the product's resolution and interpretation limits.

Common thread

The deliverable answers a project question.

These examples are not a menu of unrelated reports. They show one working method: organize the evidence, choose analysis that fits the decision, document uncertainty, and hand off something a project team can review and use.

Data with provenance

Source, date, scale, coordinate system, known gaps, and transformations stay visible.

Analysis with purpose

Each map or metric is tied to the decision rather than included only because a layer exists.

Results with limits

Planning-level evidence is kept separate from official, legal, surveyed, or engineering determinations.
