Finding data is hard.
Using it legally matters.

Open access is not always open reuse. A credible geospatial workflow needs to check coverage, currency, permissions, and gaps—not just whether a file downloads.

A problem worth solving

Data access is part of the analysis.

Research and independent demonstration work often runs into missing layers, inconsistent regional coverage, unclear rights, and datasets that permit viewing but not redistribution.

◎

Availability

Does the data exist for the target area, at the scale and time period the project needs?

≡

License & attribution

Is commercial reuse allowed? Are derivative maps, distribution, or attribution restricted?

✓

Fitness for purpose

Does the source's quality, projection, date, geometry, and completeness support the proposed claim?

Idea board / Future concept

A possible GSI Data Access Checker

The concept: a research assistant for compiling authoritative source URLs, listing file and service availability, recording license text and attribution requirements, identifying unresolved questions, and generating a human-review checklist before maps or demos are published.

QuestionWhat a future review might record
Can we obtain it?Source, geographic coverage, file format, access method, and update date
Can we use it?Named license, permitted reuse, redistribution limits, and attribution terms
Can we rely on it?Known gaps, appropriate scale, uncertainty, and QA needs
Who must decide?Outstanding permissions, contact information, and reviewer sign-off

This is an exploratory workflow concept only. No automatic licensing clearance or legal opinion is promised; source terms still require human verification and, where necessary, permission from the data owner.

Responsible demonstration work

Use data that the project can actually publish.

For open-data GIS demonstrations, GeoSite prioritizes documented sources and keeps access rights, citation requirements, and analytical uncertainty visible. Where permissions are unclear, layers are excluded or authorization is requested rather than assumed.
